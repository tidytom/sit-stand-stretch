Pushup Coach — Paper Plan

A linear strength plan that takes you from countertop pushups down to full floor pushups, one surface at a time. Test once a week, train hard and easy days, and log your sets by hand below.

Not medical advice. Stop if you feel dizzy or have pain. Ask your doctor or physical therapist before starting new exercises.

Day 1 is whatever day you start; your rest day is simply the day before your next Day 1.

  • Test + Hard (Day 1): the only day you test. Set 1 is as many pushups as you can on your current surface — that counts as your first set and your max for the week. Then do four more hard sets.
  • Hard days: ~75–80% of your max per set, 5 sets, pushed hard with good form. Transition work happens here.
  • Easy days: ~45–50% of your max, 5 clean sets, stopping with reps to spare. These build form and recovery.
  • Rest: at least ~4 minutes between all sets; a full rest day lets you recover.

The Surface Ladder (easier → harder)

Countertop → Chair / Bench → Stairs → Floor

Moving To The Next Surface

  1. When your weekly max reaches 20 on your current surface, start the next (harder) one.
  2. On hard days, do Set 1 on the new surface for max clean reps (aim 15+); keep the other four sets on your old surface at the hard target.
  3. Each time you hit 15+ reps on the new surface, move one more set down to it next hard day.
  4. When all 5 sets are on the new surface, it becomes your base — rebuild your reps until you hit 20 again.

Incorporating Floor Pushups (from Stairs)

  1. Phase 1: 1 floor pushup as the very first rep; all sets on stairs.
  2. Phase 2: 1 floor pushup to start every set; finish each set on stairs.
  3. Phase 3: 2–3 floor pushups before Set 1; finish Set 1 and all sets on stairs.
  4. Phase 4: entire Set 1 on the floor; Sets 2–5 on stairs.
  5. Then: move Set 2, then 3, 4, then 5 to the floor one at a time until every set is on the floor — full pushups.
